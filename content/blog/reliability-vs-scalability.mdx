Reliability and scalability are two of the most commonly conflated properties in distributed systems design. Engineers reach for horizontal scaling when they have a reliability problem, or obsess over fault tolerance when their real challenge is throughput.

## What Reliability Actually Means

Reliability is the probability that a system will perform its intended function without failure over a specified period. It is a property of correctness and availability — the system does what it is supposed to do, when it is supposed to do it.

## What Scalability Actually Means

Scalability is the ability of a system to handle increased load while maintaining acceptable performance. It is a property of capacity.

## Why They Get Confused

Both problems can manifest as the same symptom: the system stops working correctly under load. An overloaded system that becomes unavailable looks like a reliability failure, but the root cause is a capacity problem.

The solutions are different. Reliability problems require redundancy, circuit breakers, and graceful degradation. Scalability problems require horizontal scaling, caching, and load distribution.

## The Architectural Implication

Design for reliability first. A system that fails gracefully at capacity is more trustworthy than one that scales but fails unpredictably. Once reliability properties are established through SLOs and error budgets, then optimize for scalability.
